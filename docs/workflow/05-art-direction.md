# 5. Art direction: propose, show, let the lead choose

**Objective.** Help the lead choose with full knowledge. This is a working method, not a style: aesthetic choices remain
decisions of each project ([decision 0002](../decisions/0002-method-separate-from-aesthetics.md)).

## Roles [proven]

- **The lead** decides the intent, the references and the final choice. Their visual judgement wins over the agent's
  measurements.
- **The agent** analyses, proposes, runs the trials, measures, flags risks, and takes reversible decisions when they
  are delegated. It presents its recommendation and its reasons without mistaking them for an approval.

## Method [proven]

1. **Analyse the reference on equivalent crops** (subject, large elements, edges, materials, foreground). For each gap,
   separate what is seen from assumptions about how it was made; show the priority gaps in an **annotated
   comparison**.
2. **Identify the few interventions that will bring the most visual gain**, and start with them.
3. **Analyse each element**: size, area, depth, sharpness, what lies behind or around it, light, existing role.
4. **Give each important element a role** ([P16](../principles.md#p16)) and account for interactions: a treatment can
   enrich a neighbouring effect or make the image confusing.
5. **Make treatments reusable and combinable**: treatment presets inherited and overridden, grouped into **named
   variants** that are switched on without modifying the main scene.
6. **Propose contrasting variants**, with at least one extreme **counter-trial** ("treat everything", "almost
   nothing"). Without a counter-trial, the real preference stays invisible.
7. **Present an annotated view** (roles, treatments, composition lines) **and a preview of the whole piece in
   motion**, at final size, in every format.
8. **Treat functional constraints** (legibility of an interface laid over the image, WCAG contrast) as **checks**,
   never as arguments to tone a treatment down.
9. **Collect the choice and write it down** as it was expressed; it becomes a project decision, and a collaboration
   default if it is confirmed from one project to the next.
10. **Dose the sensitive areas** once the direction is chosen, by comparing several dosages.

## Open creative exploration [proven]

When the request is open ("no behaviour is imposed on this element"):

1. Observe the element's shape, material and integration in the source.
2. Propose **a few distinct directions**, each with a reference, its visual interest, how it fits with the existing
   work, its production cost and its render or execution cost.
3. **Define the intended effect before choosing the technique.**
4. Run short trials of the most promising ones; explicitly drop those that scatter attention (constant agitation, a
   light blotch, a decorative effect without a role).
5. Let the lead choose; keep the proposals not chosen, disabled by default.

## Pitfalls

- **Defending your recommendation against the lead's choice** ([A12](../quality/anti-patterns.md#a12)).
- **Mosaic and uniformity**: elements that are too similar look mechanical; vary dimensions, silhouettes and responses
  on purpose.
- **Copying the reference's inconsistencies**, or its text.
- **Proposing without showing motion**: a static board is not enough for an animated or interactive project.
- **Taking a suggested technique for the concept**: a remark or reference from the lead is an indication, not a
  solution to reproduce as is.
- **Lacking ambition** ([A20](../quality/anti-patterns.md#a20)): the bar is the best references of the domain, not the
  project's current state.
